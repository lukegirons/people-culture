06 Pay Distribution by Quartile
Where men and women sit across the pay spectrum · Global

Each bar shows what percentage of that gender group falls in each pay quartile — not raw counts. This corrects for the fact that we have more men than women: if distribution were perfectly equal, both bars would reach the same height in every quartile. Quartile boundaries are calculated on the global population.

What this tells us
  • 37% of women fall in the lowest pay quartile (Q1), compared to 18.5% of men. This gap is real — but it primarily reflects a seniority and representation gap (fewer women at senior levels), not a pattern of women being paid less than men in the same role.
  • In Q4 (highest pay), 27% of men and 20% of women are represented — a meaningful but not extreme difference, and largely explained by the current gender composition at Director and VP levels.
  • The company holds a Gender Equality Certification, reflecting work done on pay equity within comparable roles. The quartile data maps where the pipeline challenge remains.
  • This indicator is explicitly required by EU Directive 2023/970.

07 Methodology & Notes
How to read this dashboard
  • Compensation metric: All figures show annual base salary only (FTE-adjusted for part-time employees). Variable compensation — bonuses and on-target earnings — is not included here because it is already publicly documented on Confluence by role and level, and its structure is known to all employees. Monthly remote work allowances for fully remote employees are also excluded.
  • Geography matters: European salaries are denominated in EUR; North American salaries in USD. Compensation markets in the US/Canada are structurally higher than in Europe. Mixing the two geographies in a single average can create the appearance of large gaps that are entirely explained by geography. Use the Europe or North America toggle for within-market analysis.
  • Mean vs. median: Both are shown. The mean (average) is sensitive to extreme values; the median (midpoint) is more robust. When they diverge significantly, the median is usually the more informative figure.
  • Gap direction: A positive gap (shown in red) means men earn more on average. A negative gap (shown in green) means women earn more. Both directions are shown without editorial judgment — they reflect the current composition of each group.
  • Small groups: Levels or functions with fewer than 3 people of one gender are shown but should be interpreted with caution. Individual salaries, not structural patterns, drive the figures in these cases.
  • M1 - Associate Manager: The two employees at this level have functionally different roles (one commercial, one staff). The apparent gap reflects role differences, not compensation inequity within the same role.
  • EU Directive 2023/970: This dashboard is designed to meet the transparency and information rights obligations of the EU Pay Transparency Directive, including workforce composition, pay gap by category, and quartile distribution disclosures.
  • Data source: Bloom (Workday-based HRIS). Data exported June 15, 2026. Next scheduled update: December 2026.
  • Questions or feedback? Reach out to the People & Culture team or use the anonymous feedback form linked on the Grow Committee Confluence space.
Legal context
This dashboard is produced in line with the obligations of EU Directive 2023/970 on pay transparency, which requires employers to provide workers with information on pay levels and pay gap indicators. The right to request individual pay comparisons by gender category is available to all employees — contact People & Culture to exercise this right.
Data privacy
All data is aggregated. No individual salary information is disclosed in this dashboard. Groups with fewer than 3 people of one gender are displayed with a caution note. The dashboard is updated manually from Bloom exports twice yearly.
