01 — Context
The industrial era hangover
Most organizations are still optimized for the wrong thing.
🏭 Yesterday's priority
Eliminate errors. Maximize consistency. One deviation, one failure.
Made sense in a factory. Still dominates most orgs.
⚡ Today's real risk
Becoming irrelevant because you're not creative or flexible enough to adapt.
The biggest threat is no longer the mistake. It's the missed reinvention.
A culture of innovation is in fundamental tension with a culture of error elimination. Most organizations try to have both, and end up with neither. The question is: where do we want to sit on that dial?
Erin Meyer @ CFMT, June 2026

02 — Framework
Three ingredients for a high-performing culture
Each step unlocks the next. You can't skip ahead.
1
+ Increase talent density
Hire fewer people, but ones we're 100% convinced of. A great workplace is great colleagues, not perks.
2
+ Increase candor
Create a culture where people give and ask for honest feedback, routinely, not only in performance cycles.
3
Context, not control
Give people the context to act autonomously. Freedom doesn't reduce accountability, it creates it.
This is a continuation of the conversation we started at the 2024 company retreat. "Context, not control" was at the center of that moment too. Today we go deeper.
Erin Meyer @ CFMT, June 2026

03 — Talent density
Raising the bar, intentionally
Not a one-time decision. An ongoing organizational discipline.
🎯
Hire fewer, better
Fewer hires we're 100% convinced of, strong skills, strong fit. Not backfilling by default.
📈
Coach toward excellence
Intentional coaching, feedback, and performance evaluation, not once a year, but as a continuous practice.
High performers are energized by other high performers. The team environment itself is a retention and engagement lever, and it compounds over time.
Erin Meyer @ CFMT, June 2026

04 — Candor
We have the framework. Do we have the habit?
The DESC model is the easy part. Psychological safety and routine are the hard part.
A signal from our own data
At a TGIF earlier this year, a live pulse check showed that a significant portion of our colleagues had not asked for feedback in over three months.
A feedback ritual worth trying
Erin's recommendation: start small, pick one ritual, make it regular. The one with the highest potential ROI for us:
20'
1:1 feedback rounds across the team
Each team member schedules a short 1:1 with every other member. One thing going well. One thing to improve. At the end, everyone shares one takeaway they'll act on, and asks the team for support.
🧪 P&C Pilot
The People and Culture team is already running this as a pilot. The goal is to learn from the experience before proposing it more broadly.
Erin Meyer @ CFMT, June 2026

05 — Context and autonomy
Pyramids and trees: two types of organizations
The model that fits depends on what you're optimizing for.
🔺
The pyramid
Decisions climb up for approval. Works well in safety-critical environments where consistency equals safety.
Manufacturing, healthcare, aviation
🌳
The tree
Leaders provide context. People at every level decide autonomously. More agile, attracts and retains great talent.
Knowledge work, SaaS, product orgs
The question for us: do our people have enough context to act as informed captains? We're investing in this, but engagement and viewership of our internal business updates tell us there's still room to grow.
Erin Meyer @ CFMT, June 2026

06 — Performance
The performance equation
Team performance is shaped more by the floor than the ceiling.
Research finding
"The best predictor of how a team performs is not how great the best performer is. Most often it comes down to the lowest performance on the team."
Prof. William Felps, UNSW Sydney
Be intentional about performance
Performance evaluation is a genuine opportunity to close gaps, set direction, and grow people, not just a bureaucratic cycle.
Watch for early signals
Performance trends, positive or negative, are contagious. The earlier we act, the more we can do.
Invest in development
Coaching people toward their best is one of the highest-leverage things a manager can do.
Erin Meyer @ CFMT, June 2026

07 — Discussion
Three questions to sit with
Not a scorecard. An honest conversation.
1
On talent
Are we being intentional enough about who we hire and how we develop people? Where could we raise the bar in how we coach and evaluate?
2
On candor
What feedback isn't being given right now, and why? Are we as managers modeling the behavior we're asking of our teams?
3
On context
Do our people have enough clarity to act as informed captains? What are we not communicating, or communicating in a way that doesn't land?
This session is food for thought for your leadership practice. The goal is to leave with one concrete thing each of us will do differently.
Erin Meyer @ CFMT, June 2026
