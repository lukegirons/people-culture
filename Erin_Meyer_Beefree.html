01 — Context
The industrial era hangover
Most organizations are still optimized for the wrong thing.
🏭 Yesterday's priority
Eliminate errors. Maximize consistency. One deviation = one failure.
Made sense in a factory. Still dominates most orgs.
⚡ Today's real risk
Becoming irrelevant because you're not creative or flexible enough to adapt.
The biggest threat is no longer the mistake. It's the missed reinvention.
A culture of innovation is in fundamental tension with a culture of error elimination. Most organizations try to have both — and end up with neither. The question is: where do we want to sit on that dial?
Erin Meyer @ CFMT, June 2026

02 — Framework
Three steps to freedom & responsibility
Each step unlocks the next. You can't skip ahead.
1
+ Increase talent density
Hire fewer people, but ones we're 100% convinced of. A great workplace is great colleagues — not perks.
2
+ Increase candor
Create a culture where people give and ask for honest feedback — routinely, not only in performance cycles.
3
− Remove decision-making controls
Give people the context to act. Move from pyramid to tree. Freedom doesn't reduce accountability — it creates it.
Adapted from Erin Meyer & Reed Hastings, "No Rules Rules" (Netflix Culture Model)
Erin Meyer @ CFMT, June 2026

03 — Talent density
Raising the bar — intentionally
Not a one-time decision. An ongoing organizational discipline.
🎯
Hire fewer, better
Fewer hires we're 100% convinced of — strong skills, strong fit. Not backfilling by default.
📈
Coach toward excellence
Intentional coaching, feedback, and performance evaluation — not once a year, but as a continuous practice.
🔁
The Keeper Test
"If this person told me they were leaving for a similar role, would I fight hard to keep them?"
High performers are energized by other high performers. The team environment itself is a retention and engagement lever — and it compounds over time.
Erin Meyer @ CFMT, June 2026

04 — Candor
We have the framework. Do we have the habit?
The DESC model is the easy part. Psychological safety and routine are the hard part.
A signal from our own data
At a TGIF earlier this year, a live pulse check showed that a significant portion of our colleagues had not asked for feedback in over three months.
Three feedback rituals from Erin's session — practical, low-lift
1
20-minute 1:1 feedback rounds
Each team member schedules a short 1:1 with every other member. One thing going well, one thing to improve. At the end, everyone shares one takeaway they'll act on.
2
Quick feedback rounds in team meetings
Pairs share one thing the other does well + one improvement idea in 5 minutes. At the next meeting: report back on what you worked on. If done regularly, feedback becomes the baseline.
3
360° feedback dinners (Netflix-style)
The whole team takes turns receiving feedback publicly. Sounds scary. Works because when everyone's watching, people are generous and supportive — no one's singled out.
💡 A proposal from Enrica: run a small team feedback circle pilot — 1:1s across the team, one area to work on, share intentions at the next monthly meeting and ask the team for support.
Erin Meyer @ CFMT, June 2026

05 — Context & autonomy
Pyramids & trees: two types of organizations
The model that fits depends on what you're optimizing for.
🔺
The pyramid
Decisions climb up for approval. Works well in safety-critical environments where consistency = safety.
Manufacturing, healthcare, aviation
🌳
The tree
Leaders provide context. People at every level decide autonomously. More agile, attracts and retains top talent.
Knowledge work, SaaS, product orgs
Structuring Massimo's monthly business updates was a step in the right direction — but viewership is still lower than we'd want. The information exists. The question is: does it reach people in a way they can actually act on?
Erin Meyer @ CFMT, June 2026

06 — Performance
The performance equation
The ceiling isn't set by your best person. The floor is set by your lowest performance.
≈ 50%
reduction in team performance observed when a low performer is on the team
Prof. William Felps — UNSW Sydney
Apply the Keeper Test consistently
Not as a thought experiment — as a real input to how we manage and develop our people.
Take performance evaluation seriously
Not as a bureaucratic cycle, but as a genuine opportunity to close gaps and set direction.
Watch for early signals
Poor performance is especially contagious. Address it before it spreads.
Erin Meyer @ CFMT, June 2026

07 — Discussion
Three questions to sit with
Not a scorecard. An honest conversation.
1
On talent
Are we holding the bar consistently? Where might we be tolerating situations we shouldn't — either in how we hire or in how we coach and develop?
2
On candor
What feedback isn't being given right now — and why? Are we as managers modeling the behavior we're asking of our teams?
3
On context
Do our people have enough clarity to act as informed captains? What are we not communicating — or communicating in a way that doesn't land?
This session is food for thought for your leadership practice. The goal is to leave with one concrete thing each of us will do differently.
Erin Meyer @ CFMT, June 2026
