01 — Our data
What Peakon is telling us
A few comments from the past months that made us stop and think.

"Most changes are communicated ex post and the rationale behind it is almost never clear."

"I see a lot of short-term strategy but very little visibility into the medium term."

"Sometimes I do not know what I am supposed to do. Planning execution of the strategy seems poor."

These comments sting a little. And they should, because they're telling us something real, and that we've been trying to address, but still, there's more work to do.

Worth being precise: as we shared in the last Peakon Digest, what's driving these scores is not a single thing. It's a mix of uncertainty about financial results, discomfort with the pace of change, and in some cases disagreement with specific decisions. These are different problems that call for different responses.

02 — The problem
Work whiplash
It has a name. And it's not unique to Beefree.
What is it?
Work whiplash is what happens when change occurs without communication. It's the gap between what leadership knows and what everyone else knows, and it's where wasted time, frustration, and disengagement get manufactured.
"One of the most demoralizing things that can happen to someone at work is spending weeks pushing something uphill, only to find out the strategy changed, the project no longer matters, or another team was quietly working on the same thing."

Molly Graham, Glue Club
Not malicious
It happens because leadership moves fast and communication is harder than it looks, not because anyone meant harm.
A threshold moment
When a company passes ~50 people, the default must flip: from "people know" to "people probably don't know."

03 — The human side
What it feels like, and what to do with it
Being nimble is a strength. But we need to pair that ask with honesty about what change costs.
Uncertainty, shifting priorities, decisions you didn't see coming: these feelings are normal. You might remember Bob from the retreat, that anxious feeling that chews on your leg when things move fast. Bob is not you. Naming it makes it manageable.
Shared Pool of Meaning
We each enter every conversation with our own experiences, opinions, and blind spots. When we work in the open and make it genuinely safe to contribute, that pool grows, and the better the decisions that come out of it.
What this asks of all of us
Don't sit with the uncertainty alone. Ask. Share your perspective. The goal isn't to pretend the fog isn't there, it's to walk into it together.

04 — The response
Context, not control
One question. Ask it every time.
"Who is actively working on something that this affects, and do they know already?"
Not at the next meeting. Not in the quarterly update. Today.
We've introduced this before
We introduced the concept of "context, not control" at the 2024 retreat, and it's always good to remember it, because the data says we need to.
A shared responsibility
This isn't only on leadership. Each of us, when we know something a colleague doesn't and it affects them, has the responsibility to close that gap.

05 — What we're doing
Work in progress
It's not solved. But we're working on it.
1
Monthly business updates
We're working to make them less one-way and more of a real conversation, better engagement, better viewership, more interaction.
2
Explaining the why, not just the what
When decisions are made or priorities shift, we want to communicate the reasoning, not just the outcome.
3
You
Next time something changes in your world, tell the people it affects. Don't wait for the all-hands. Work whiplash is everyone's problem to solve.
Work whiplash is, at its core, wasted time. And people's time is the most valuable thing we have as an organization.

"If our strategy is not clear, ask for clarifications. If you disagree with elements of our strategy, voice your opinion."
