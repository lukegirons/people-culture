Work Whiplash

Change is inevitable. Whiplash is not.

Source
Molly Graham, Glue Club
Presented by
Luca Gironi
01 — The problem
Work whiplash

"One of the most demoralizing things that can happen to someone at work is spending weeks pushing something uphill, only to find out the strategy changed, the project no longer matters, or another team was quietly working on the same thing."

It happens when change occurs without enough communication.
It's the gap between what some people know and what others don't, at every level.
Molly Graham, Glue Club
01 / 05
02 — Why it happens
It's not malicious. It's not unique to us.
It happens at every level: leadership, project leads, peers.
Leadership
A decision is made in a meeting. The teams affected don't hear about it until it's too late.
Project leads
A pivot happens inside a project. Not everyone on the team knows who decided what, or why.
Peers
Two people are working on the same thing without knowing it. A doc gets updated without telling anyone.
The cost
Wasted time. Disengagement. The feeling that your work doesn't matter.
Two concrete examples from our own experience: indirect channel, SDK and RGE Studio.
On RGE Studio: the whiplash wasn't about the green light itself, but about not clarifying that what was approved was the continuation of an exploration, not a product decision.

03 — The response
Context, not control
Leading by context is what helps us prevent work whiplash.
🔺
The pyramid
Decisions climb up for approval. People wait for direction. Information flows top-down, slowly.
🌳
The tree
Leaders provide context. People at every level decide autonomously. They feel like owners, not executors.
With the right context, people make autonomous decisions at every level.
We introduced this at the 2024 retreat. The data says it's worth revisiting.
Erin Meyer, #PeopleManagementForum
03 / 05
04 — What we can do
What is good context?
A reminder of how we work best, at every level.
Leadership
Share what's discussed in management meetings when it impacts your team's work or projects.
Project drivers
Clarify who drives, approves, is responsible, and who just needs to be informed. Document as you go.
Everyone
Give proactive updates. Don't wait to be asked.
Everyone
Read your Slack channels. The information is there, but only if you go looking.
Everyone
Raise your hand when something doesn't make sense. Ask. Be vocal.

05 — To close
This is a shared responsibility.
Change is inevitable.
Whiplash is not.
Every time a decision is made or a priority shifts, ask yourself:
Do the people affected by this already know?
Not at the next meeting. Not in the quarterly update. Today.
"If our strategy is not clear, ask for clarifications. If you disagree with elements of our strategy, voice your opinion."
